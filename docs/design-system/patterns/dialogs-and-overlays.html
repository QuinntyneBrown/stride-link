Pattern

Dialogs and overlays

The page draws no dialog of its own: the browser owns the chooser and leave prompt, the Stop bar is a fixed layer no overlay may cover, and connection lost stays until dismissed.

Overview

Stride Link draws no dialog of its own in the shipped page. The overlays a tester meets are:

  • The browser's Bluetooth chooser, opened only by a Connect press (L2-006). Its look and wording belong to Chrome or Edge; both mock sets draw it for flow context only, with .native and .device-list.
  • The browser's leave-page confirmation, raised by beforeunload while log entries are unexported (L2-041). Drawn in set B only; its wording belongs to the browser.
  • The Stop bar, a fixed layer (--z-stop-bar) above the page but below every overlay layer. It is not a dialog and never traps focus.
  • Connection lost (L2-048): the one Stride Link message that must stay until dismissed. The three sources disagree on its form; see below.

When to use

Open decision: connection lost. The owner has not chosen between the mock sets. All three forms show the same L2 sentence and stay until dismissed.

FormSourceForAgainst
Alert dialog (role="alertdialog", modal, focus moved in)Set AImpossible to miss; offers Export log beside DismissTraps focus while the tester may need the log; a modal over a safety page hides the context
Full-width banner under the top bar (.alert.banner)Set BVisible at the top at every width; page stays usableCan scroll out of view on a long page
Inline danger alert first in the primary columnShipped (#connection-lost)Same as the banner, inside the content width; first in reading orderSame scroll caveat as the banner

Whichever form is chosen, these rules hold: the sentence is verbatim, the message uses role="alert", it does not auto-dismiss, and it appears only after gattserverdisconnected while the belt was active.

Drawers, popovers, menus and tooltips are not part of this system: nothing in either set needs them.

Stacking order

LayerTokenValue
Sticky top bar--z-sticky
Stop bar--z-stop-bar
Backdrop--z-overlay
Dialog--z-modal
Toast region (set A)--z-toast

A modal above the Stop bar hides Stop. That is why the shipped page has no modal while the belt can be active, and why any future dialog shown with the belt active must not cover the Stop bar and must not stop Escape from sending Stop.

Responsive behaviour

  • Dialogs are bottom sheets below 640 px (top corners rounded, full width) and centred cards from 640 px; the footer stays sticky so actions remain reachable.
  • The banner (set B) is full width at every size; the shipped inline alert takes the primary column's width.
  • The Stop bar is full width below 768 px and centres a 24 rem button from 768 px; it stays fixed at every width from 320 to 1920 px (L2-045).

Accessibility

  • Set A's dialog follows the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices Alert and Message Dialogs pattern: role="alertdialog", aria-modal, labelled by the title, described by the L2 sentence; focus moves to Dismiss, Escape closes, focus returns, the page behind is inert.
  • The banner and the shipped alert use role="alert" and take no focus; Dismiss is the next Tab stop in reading order.
  • Escape always sends Stop while the belt is active, from any focus, inputs included (L2-045, captured on the document). No overlay may consume Escape first.
  • The Stop bar is in the Tab order, operable by Enter and Space, at least 64 × 64 px, and never covered by the page (L2-045, L2-064).
  • Backdrop and panel animations are removed under reduced motion (L2-063).

Content

  • Connection lost, verbatim: Connection lost. If the belt is moving, use the treadmill's stop button or safety key.
  • Set A's dialog adds a title, Connection lost, and a body line, Stride Link can no longer send Stop. The run's log and verdict are kept. Neither is in L2.
  • Dismiss is the only action that is always offered. Export log is offered by the verdict card in every form, so the dialog's copy of it is optional.
  • Do not reword browser dialogs; draw them only to show the flow and label them as browser-native.
